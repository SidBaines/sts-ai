# Joint run 2: out-of-combat PPO with repeated gated combat refits, against a same-seed control

**Status:** finished 23 October. **Verdict:** better than its same-seed control under the rule fixed in advance (1,071 against 998 of 4,096 unused worlds, p = 0.022), but the whole gain comes from the refitted combat head; there is no sign of co-adaptation.

## Question

The [first joint run](2026-10-22-joint-loop.md) beat its control (945
against 877 of 4,096 unused worlds, p = 0.029) with only one combat refit,
because its orchestrator hung after a disk-space stop. A two-by-two split
the gain into the refit itself (+1.5 points) and a borderline co-adaptation
term (+1.8 points, z = 1.96): the out-of-combat head that trained with the
new combat head played best with it. Does a run with several refits, and a
control that differs only through those refits, show a clearer gain?
Sid asked for this run on 22 October.

## Design

- **Joint arm** (`joint2_20261022`): out-of-combat PPO from the champion
  (`champion_af2fb9f1f260.pt`) with exactly the fourth continued training's
  settings (24 iterations of 3,072 games, lambda 1, weight decay 0,
  learning rate 0.0003, potion filter, detached critic, normalised value
  targets, entropy bonus 0.01 on strategic menus, evaluation every 4
  iterations, training seed 5), plus `--combat-follow`, starting from the
  champion's own combat head (the round 2 student). Code: a frozen checkout
  of ws/nonllm whose trainer differs from the control's only by the
  combat-follow option.
- **Control:** the [fourth continued training](2026-10-22-continued-training-4.md)
  (same start, same seed, same settings, combat head fixed). Until the first
  refit is adopted the two runs see the same training worlds; whether they
  are identical step for step will be checked from the first iteration's
  statistics.
- **Loop** (`experiments/2026-10-22-joint-run-2/joint_loop.py`): as in the
  first run (each cycle labels 1,000 new worlds with the search teacher
  from the trainer's newest checkpoint, fits two students on all cycle
  corpora plus the round 2 and round 3 corpora, and gates them on 2,048
  worlds from 101,000,000,000; a student is adopted if it wins at least as
  many games and its mean floor is at least as high). New: a fit or
  evaluation that does not finish successfully is retried once and
  otherwise skipped; every step has a timeout; the loop pauses below 15 GiB
  free disk; a heartbeat file shows what it is doing; it stops when the
  trainer finishes. Label worlds: 90,500,700,000 plus 10,000 per cycle.
- **Decision rule, fixed in advance:** the joint run's and the control's
  final checkpoints, and the champion, play 4,096 unused worlds from
  105,000,000,000; the joint run is *Better* if it wins more than the
  control with exact McNemar p < 0.05.
- **Secondary, also fixed in advance:** the same two-by-two as in the first
  run on those worlds (each final out-of-combat head with each final combat
  head); the interaction (does the joint run's out-of-combat head gain more
  from its own combat head than the control's does) is reported with its
  interval, as evidence about co-adaptation. Number of refits adopted, gate
  margins, and the cost in hours and labelling compute are also reported.
- **Approval:** Sid, 22 October ("build the robust orchestrator and queue
  the proper joint run after the fourth continued training, from the
  champion with the same seed and config so the fourth round is its
  control").

## Results

**Main comparison (rule fixed in advance): the joint run beats its control.**
4,096 unused worlds from 105,000,000,000, environment 0.1.5.1, study
protocol, paired by world:

| Player | Wins | vs control | Mean floor |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Control: fourth continued training | 998 (24.4%) | | 40.64 |
| Joint run 2 (two refits adopted) | 1,071 (26.1%) | +1.8 points [+0.3, +3.3] (discordant 460/533, p = 0.022) | +0.73 [+0.33, +1.13] |
| Starting point (champion) | 862 (21.0%) | | |

The loop adopted two refits (cycle 0 after iteration 8: gate 483 against 449
of 2,048 worlds, p = 0.09; cycle 1 after iteration 16: 532 against 495, p =
0.07); a third was fitting when the trainer finished. Training is
deterministic: the first iteration matched the control's exactly.

**Two-by-two (fixed in advance): the gain is the refitted combat head, with
no co-adaptation.** Same worlds, wins of 4,096:

| | Champion's combat head (round 2 student) | Joint run's final combat head |
|---|---:|---:|
| Control's out-of-combat head | 998 | 1,101 |
| Joint run's out-of-combat head | 967 | 1,071 |

- Combat refit, averaged over both out-of-combat heads: +2.5 points [+1.5, +3.6].
- Out-of-combat heads: −0.7 points [−1.8, +0.3].
- Interaction (co-adaptation): +0.0 points [−1.9, +1.9]. The first run's
  borderline +1.8 did not replicate.
- The control's out-of-combat head with the joint run's combat head won
  1,101 (+2.5 points [+1.1, +4.0] against the control; p = 0.001; floor +1.32
  [+0.97, +1.68]; Act 2 boss pass +4.8 points [+3.2, +6.4]), the best
  player found; it was picked after seeing the four cells, so it is being
  confirmed on 4,096 fresh worlds from 107,000,000,000. Decision rule, fixed
  before those games: *Better* than the fourth continued training alone if
  it wins more with exact McNemar p < 0.05.
- **Confirmed** (worlds from 107,000,000,000): the combination won 1,083
  against the fourth continued training's 952 (+3.2 points [+1.8, +4.6];
  discordant 368/499, p < 0.001; floor +1.68 [+1.32, +2.03]; Act 2 boss
  pass +5.6 points [+4.0, +7.3]). The joint run's own final checkpoint won
  1,096 there (+3.5 points [+2.0, +5.0] against 952, p < 0.001), with no
  clear difference from the combination (1,096 against 1,083, p = 0.69).

**Where the combat head's gain comes from (exploratory, same games).** How
the 4,096 games of each cell end:

| Games ending at | Control's out-of-combat head, old combat head | same, new combat head | Joint run's out-of-combat head, old combat head | same, new combat head |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Act 1, before the boss | 112 | 112 | 143 | 129 |
| Act 1 boss | 233 | 171 | 287 | 232 |
| Act 2, before the boss | 271 | 218 | 273 | 214 |
| Act 2 boss | 1,090 | 972 | 1,099 | 965 |
| Act 3, before the boss | 258 | 264 | 277 | 253 |
| Act 3 boss | 1,134 | 1,258 | 1,050 | 1,232 |
| Win | 998 | 1,101 | 967 | 1,071 |

- The new combat head passes the Act 1 boss (94.2% to 95.7% of those reaching
  it) and the Act 2 boss (68.7% to 73.0%) more often and loses fewer Act 2
  hallway fights, so more games reach the Act 3 boss (2,132 to 2,359), where
  its pass rate is unchanged (46.8% and 46.7%).
- It drinks potions more: 6.1% of chances against 4.9% (9.9 against 8.8 per
  game), in line with the teacher's heavier potion use.
- The joint run's out-of-combat head upgrades at rest sites more (59% against
  42% of rest sites with at least half HP), reaches the Act 1 boss with less
  HP (73.5% against 79.4% of maximum) and dies there more; it does slightly
  better at the Act 3 boss, which roughly cancels out.

## Conclusion

Gated combat refits on the current player's own fights work: each adopted
refit improved the agent, and together they add about 2.5 points of wins.
Training the out-of-combat head alongside them gave nothing extra: an
out-of-combat head trained with the old combat head does as well with the
new one. So the useful part of the joint loop is the refit-and-gate cycle,
which can run beside ordinary out-of-combat training and be spliced in
afterwards, rather than true joint training.

Caveats:
- One run per arm (with training determinism, the arms differ only through
  the refits).
- Gate decisions used reused gate worlds and lenient acceptance (any win
  gain without a floor loss); both adopted students also improved the
  floor.
- The best combination was chosen after the fact, then confirmed on fresh
  worlds.
